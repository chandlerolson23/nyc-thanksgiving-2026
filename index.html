Choose your own New York

Four adults. Four available dates. A collection of events, active experiences, city classics and food—not a prescribed route or daily timetable. Browse by interest, then use each option’s availability and booking details.

How to use the collection

Pick what sounds fun: skyline rides, games, animals, holiday displays, architecture, food or art. Nearby pairings are suggestions, never a sequence to follow. Visit lengths are planning estimates.

Scope: a broad, researched selection—not a claim to list every event in New York. Some smaller performances and holiday operations have not released final details. Later announcements belong in the watchlist, not the confirmed collection.

Price and availability labels

Free: no ordinary admission charge for the option described. Under $20: published adult admission or the specific activity described is below $20—not $20 or less. Transport, meals and optional extras are separate.

Harbor air & the Manhattan skyline

Independent harbor and waterfront choices, with no assigned day or route. Weather and the amount of walking the group wants should guide the selection.

$0 FOR ALL FOUR

Staten Island Ferry

Whitehall Terminal, 4 South Street, Manhattan

Available: Nov 24–27 · Thanksgiving uses holiday service

A working commuter ferry—not a narrated tour—crosses New York Harbor. You see Lower Manhattan, shipping traffic and the Statue of Liberty from a distance. It does not stop at Liberty or Ellis Island.

Plan: approximately 25 minutes each way; allow 75–100 minutes with boarding and the return connection. Everyone must get off at Staten Island and reboard for the return. Aim for a daylight ride; enclosed areas provide a break from wind. No ticket or reservation.

Thanksgiving uses the weekend schedule, normally half-hourly. Check same-day service for weather changes. Ignore street sellers offering a ticket for this free ferry. Pair with the nearby free Smithsonian museum only if the group genuinely wants an indoor stop.

$0 · NO BOOKING

Walk the Brooklyn Bridge

Pedestrian promenade between Lower Manhattan and Brooklyn

Available: Nov 24–27 · ordinary public access, weather permitting

Walk through the bridge’s stone towers and web of suspension cables, with city and river views in both directions. It is an experience in itself, not just a photo stop. Allow 45–60 minutes one way at a relaxed photo pace; add time to reach the waterfront.

From Manhattan, use the pedestrian entrance near City Hall/Park Row. From Brooklyn, the Tillary Street/Boerum Place entrance offers the main ramp approach; consult the access link for alternatives. Walking from Brooklyn toward Manhattan puts the skyline ahead; walking the other way finishes near DUMBO after an additional walk.

Wear wind-blocking layers and comfortable shoes. Take the subway back rather than committing to a second crossing. If it is wet, icy or unpleasantly windy, shorten the plan to a waterfront visit or indoor alternative.

Attractions / ACTIVE & MEMORABLE

A park above the streets—and a ride above the river

Three independent choices: an elevated park, a sculpted river park and an aerial tram. Each can be enjoyed on its own; there is no assigned sightseeing day.

$0 · WALK IN

The High Line

Manhattan’s West Side, from Gansevoort Street toward 34th Street

Available: Nov 24–27 · regular November hours 7 am–10 pm

A former elevated freight-rail line is now a pedestrian park. Walk above street level past planted beds, changing public art and unusual close-up views of buildings. Old tracks and lookout areas make it more interesting than a standard sidewalk walk.

Allow 45–75 minutes for a selected stretch, not necessarily the entire park. A useful route is the 30th Street area south toward Gansevoort Street. Published November hours are 7 am–10 pm. No ordinary entry ticket. Check the official elevator-status link if anyone needs step-free access. Bicycles and recreational scooters are not allowed on the park.

Little Island and the Whitney are nearby options, not required next stops.

Little Island

Pier 55, Hudson River Park, near West 13th Street

Available: Nov 24–27 · regular November hours 6 am–9 pm

An unusually shaped public park rises from the Hudson on tulip-like concrete supports. Follow winding paths up to overlooks for river and skyline views. It is a short exploratory walk with good photo angles—not an amusement park.
